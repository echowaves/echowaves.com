# Narration worksheet — the three Key Flows

EXPERIENCE.md → Key Flows is the only section of the spine I wrote from inference rather than evidence, and it is marked `[NEEDS YOUR NARRATION]` for that reason. You queued this item first, so here is exactly what I need. Answer in fragments; "never happened" and "no idea" are both useful answers — a guess written down as a guess is honest, a guess written as fact is the thing this whole site exists to avoid.

Answer format per line: `1.2 true / false / never-watched — what actually happened`.

## Flow 1 · Priya, VP Engineering, 25 minutes between meetings

I invented her from the sources' stated audience ("engineering leaders evaluating the work"). Everything below is my guess about a stranger.

1. **Is she real?** Has anyone actually evaluated you this way — a hiring manager, a VP, a conference contact who said "I looked at your site"? One named instance beats three archetypes.
2. **Does she arrive from LinkedIn?** The site assumes a social referrer. Do people actually click through from LinkedIn posts, or do they arrive from a search result, a calendar invite, or you naming the domain in a call?
3. **The Name block.** When someone has reacted to the site in real life, what did they quote back? I wrote that she "stops at *installs a delivery system a team can run without the author*" — has anyone ever stopped at that line, or do they stop somewhere else?
4. **Does she go to `/results`?** Do people actually click through to check your numbers, or do they read the home page and form an opinion there? If nobody has ever gone to `/results`, that section is currently built for an audience with no evidence of existing.
5. **The climax I wrote** ("each figure names its source") assumes a reader who notices sourcing. Has anyone ever remarked on a source, or asked where a number came from? If not — do you still want the site written for that reader, or for the one who doesn't look?
6. **"Writes about a specific figure she wants argued with."** Have you ever actually received that message? This beat is the site's whole theory of conversion, so I'd like it to be true.

## Flow 2 · Ravi, recruiter, 90 seconds

1. **Do recruiters land here at all?** If you've had inbound interest since the domain existed, what did they actually look at?
2. **Ninety seconds** is a guess. Does that match anything real — a recruiter who called after glancing at the site, a message that quoted something from it?
3. **"Clicks `builds` to confirm he actually ships."** Do people go to `/builds`, or do they go straight to your GitHub / the app stores from a link you sent them?
4. **"Live store links, not screenshots."** Is that the thing that lands with non-technical readers, or is it the thing only *you* care about? Be blunt — I'd rather write this flow for an audience of one honest reader than three invented ones.
5. **Does a recruiter ever leave without writing?** If so, what do you think stopped them? That answer is more useful to the site than the success story.

## Flow 3 · Dmitry, six months from now

This one is yours alone and the shortest to settle.

1. **Would you actually run `npm run verify`,** or would you look at the deployed page on your phone and judge it by eye? (Both are legitimate; only one of them is what the spine currently promises.)
2. **What breaks in six months?** Which specific sentence do you expect to go stale first — a product status, a version number, an open-source figure? Tell me and I'll make that the thing the machinery guards, rather than guarding everything evenly.
3. **Who else runs it?** If nobody but you ever runs `npm run verify`, is the verifier a promise to a reader or a promise to yourself? The spine says both; I'd like it to say one on purpose.

## What I'll do with the answers

Rewrite all three flows as observed sessions, then re-derive the **climax beat** of each — those three sentences are what the page structure is optimised for, so if a flow changes, a component's priority may change with it. Anything you mark "no idea" stays in the spine explicitly labelled unverified rather than being smoothed into a plausible narrative.

If there is no evidence for Flow 1 or 2 at all, the honest rewrite is one flow — yours — plus a note that the audience is assumed. That is a worse marketing document and a better engineering document, which is roughly the trade-off this site keeps making.
