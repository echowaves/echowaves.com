Analogical thinking — “this is like what?”
Analogy 01 · EX 099 · L117

This is like what? A ship's pilot: not the crew, not the owner, but the licensed stranger who comes aboard for the hard channel and signs the log. Site language: 'I come aboard for the hard part.' Instantly differentiated role definition.

Analogy 02 · EX 100 · L118

This is like a hospital infection-control lead: success is measured in things that stopped happening. Idea: publish absence metrics — defects that never shipped, incidents prevented, outages avoided by gates.

Analogy 03 · EX 101 · L119

This is like an orchestra conductor who also tunes instruments: the transformation leader must be able to do the boring mechanical work. Idea: show tuning (real PRs he personally merged, real config files) alongside conducting (org design).

Analogy 04 · EX 102 · L120

This is like a mountain guide: paid for judgment about weather, not for climbing ability. Idea: publish 'calls I made that were wrong and how I knew' — judgment under uncertainty is the product.

Analogy 05 · EX 103 · L121

This is like a forensic accountant brought in after the audit: the buyer wants someone who will tell them their number is wrong before a regulator does. Idea: offer an adversarial baseline review as the entry-level engagement.

Analogy 06 · EX 104 · L122

This is like a restorer of paintings: consent to do less than possible, reversible interventions, documentation of every touch. Idea: 'reversibility' as a selling point for agentic change in conservative orgs — his regulated background makes it credible.

Analogy 07 · EX 105 · L123

This is like a translator between two languages engineering leaders cannot speak to each other (business cadence and agent capability). Idea: the site publishes literal translation tables — 'what the CTO says / what it means / what I change'.

Analogy 08 · EX 106 · L124

Final analogy: a luthier, not a factory. One at a time, measured by how it sounds in someone else's hands. Site scale consequence: keep capacity deliberately small and say so — scarcity framed as craft, not ego.
